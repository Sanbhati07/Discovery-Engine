  <h1>Photo Retrieval Discovery Engine — Findings from 250 Real Reviews</h1>
  <p class="sub">
    This page shows the actual output of the discovery engine after analyzing 250 real, recent Google Photos
    Play Store reviews. It's a static snapshot so it works for anyone, anywhere, with no sign-in required.
    For the live interactive version (paste your own reviews and get a fresh AI analysis), see the
    <a href="https://claude.ai/artifact/Nd764ScX8F5d6MthGM21cF" target="_blank">interactive tool</a> (needs a free Claude account).
  </p>

  <div class="card">
    <h2>Data quality — an honest note</h2>
    <div class="stat-row">
      <div class="stat"><div class="num">250</div><div class="label">reviews collected</div></div>
      <div class="stat"><div class="num">28</div><div class="label">genuinely about retrieval (~11%)</div></div>
      <div class="stat"><div class="num">0</div><div class="label">fabricated — all real, sourced</div></div>
    </div>
    <p class="sub" style="font-size:0.85rem;">
      The keyword filter used to collect these reviews was intentionally broad (matched words like "update",
      "album", "memory"), which pulled in a lot of reviews that turn out to be unrelated — app crashes,
      subscription complaints, accidental photo deletion, layout changes. Only the 28 reviews below are actually
      about failing to find a remembered photo. The themes and counts on this page are based on those 28, not
      all 250. This is a known limitation of keyword-based filtering; a next iteration would use an AI relevance
      check before clustering, not just keyword matching.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <h2>What the 28 relevant reviews show</h2>

    <div class="theme" style="border-left-color: var(--stage-other);">
      <span class="badge">Stage 1 · Expressing</span>
      <h3>No examples found in this dataset</h3>
      <div class="count">0 of 28</div>
      <p class="sub" style="font-size:0.85rem; margin:0;">
        A real finding, not an oversight: app reviews are written after someone has already tried and failed to
        search, so they don't capture the earlier moment of struggling to put a fuzzy memory into words. That
        moment can only really be captured through direct user interviews — which is exactly what Part 3 of this
        project is for.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="theme" style="border-left-color: var(--stage2);">
      <span class="badge">Stage 2 · Understanding</span>
      <h3>Search fails to use the clue the person actually gave it</h3>
      <div class="count">7 of 28</div>
      <ul class="evidence">
        <li>Searched a work address to find related photos; got no results at all.</li>
        <li>Searched "car engine" among 100+ repair photos; only 15 came up, AI often replied "none."</li>
        <li>Wanted to label and search for a specific person's face; the option doesn't work.</li>
        <li>A redesign removed the ability to browse by face-group from the search screen.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <div class="theme" style="border-left-color: var(--stage3);">
      <span class="badge">Stage 3 · Judging</span>
      <h3>Results don't carry the context needed to tell photos apart</h3>
      <div class="count">3 of 28</div>
      <ul class="evidence">
        <li>Wants photos grouped by date/event rather than topic, to distinguish similar occasions.</li>
        <li>Lost the ability to select all photos from one specific date or month in a single tap.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <div class="theme" style="border-left-color: var(--stage4);">
      <span class="badge">Stage 4 · Recovering</span>
      <h3>No good way to narrow down or regain a working search</h3>
      <div class="count">6 of 28</div>
      <ul class="evidence">
        <li>Wants a way to hide unrelated local photos so only backed-up ones show while searching.</li>
        <li>New finding: search now requires turning on cloud Backup first — locked behind an unrelated setting.</li>
        <li>Says visually scanning for a photo now "gives a migraine" since search is unreliable.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <div class="theme" style="border-left-color: var(--stage5);">
      <span class="badge">Stage 5 · Trusting — the largest, most consistent pattern</span>
      <h3>People believe the AI search is worse on purpose, and stop relying on it</h3>
      <div class="count">8 of 28</div>
      <ul class="evidence">
        <li>Believes classic keyword search was deliberately weakened to push people toward the AI assistant.</li>
        <li>Calls the AI photo assistant "useless" and "worthless" for search.</li>
        <li>Reports deceptive AI pop-ups appearing right where the search icon used to be.</li>
        <li>Multiple long-time users say search quality has declined since AI features were added.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <h2>What do "Stage 1–5" mean?</h2>
    <div class="legend-item"><strong>Stage 1 · Expressing —</strong> the person can't put a fuzzy memory into words specific enough to search with.<div class="q">From: "Is the user unable to express what they remember?"</div></div>
    <div class="legend-item"><strong>Stage 2 · Understanding —</strong> a real clue was given, but the search failed to read or use it correctly.<div class="q">From: "Does Google Photos fail to understand the clues they provide?"</div></div>
    <div class="legend-item"><strong>Stage 3 · Judging —</strong> results came back, but the person can't tell which one is right.<div class="q">From: "Are potentially relevant results difficult to evaluate?"</div></div>
    <div class="legend-item"><strong>Stage 4 · Recovering —</strong> the first search failed, with no easy way to refine or retry.<div class="q">From: "Does the user struggle to refine an unsuccessful search?"</div></div>
    <div class="legend-item"><strong>Stage 5 · Trusting —</strong> an AI search feature exists, but people distrust it and revert to old habits.<div class="q">Added from evidence — not one of the original questions, but the strongest pattern found.</div></div>
  </div>

  <div class="footer-note">

    library, filtered to English-language reviews mentioning search/retrieval-adjacent keywords, then manually
    reviewed for actual relevance. Full source code and the collection script are in this repository.
